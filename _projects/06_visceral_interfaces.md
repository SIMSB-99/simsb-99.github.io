**Topics:** Eye tracking, VR privacy, privacy notices, visceral interfaces, user studies

## Abstract

Eye tracking supports interaction, rendering optimization, and adaptive experiences in VR, but gaze data can also reveal identity, behavior, and sensitive personal attributes. This project designed three visceral, in-experience interfaces intended to make eye-tracking data collection and its implications more understandable than conventional terms-of-service notices. A user study evaluated how people perceived these interfaces. Although some visualizations could be distracting, participants generally found them informative and supported making such privacy-awareness mechanisms available, even when not enabled continuously by default.
